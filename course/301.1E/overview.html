40 cards · 20 merchants

A distributed attack
looks ordinary.

Intended behavior: detect the pattern, challenge matching attempts, notify merchants and support release and expiry.

arula03 / The heart of Define

Source-based technical authoring

A good spec starts with
a consequential question.

The authoring skill reads the supplied materials and builds its questionnaire from the feature. The engineer investigates the choices, accepts the decisions and reviews the resulting wording.

“What must stay consistent when a bank challenge completes on another replica?”

01

Read

Load inputs and drafts. Confirm the understanding with source references.

02

Investigate

Ask one consequential question. Compare evidence, options and tradeoffs.

03

Decide

Record the accepted answer, rationale, owner and affected contracts.

04

Review

Inspect the proposed diff. Accept wording, update coverage and continue.

Five rounds of investigation

01Shared lifecycle

Challenge identity, persistence, trusted completion, retries, cancellation, expiry and capture races.

02Integration + UX

Merchant facts, typed checkout outcomes, held states and analyst actions.

03Detector reliability

Cross-replica publication, outages, release, notifications and history.

04Policy + privacy

Risk owners, safe reasons, retained data and permitted narrative inputs.

05Acceptance + rollout

Observable stories, measurement, load and failure tests, cutover and rollback.

Ownership is part of the design. Engineering decides within the supplied constraints. Product, Design, Risk and Legal keep their decisions and unresolved dependencies.

arula05 / Quality and evidence

Read the finding. Follow the source.

Use the checks to sharpen
engineering judgment.

Validate compares the supplied specifications. Audit checks an RFC against its template and linked context. Every substantive finding returns to the actual documents and their owners.

speed validate specs/ speed audit specs/tech/adaptive-auth/1-high-value-traveller.md speed audit specs/tech/adaptive-auth/2-card-testing.md

Audit's four levels

01 / STRUCTURE

Is it complete?

Template headings, meaningful sections, placeholders and resolved links.

02 / SUBSTANCE

Can it be built?

Defined contracts, examples, states, decisions and observable tests.

03 / CONSISTENCY

Does it agree?

Requirements, linked PRDs and contracts across the RFC family.

04 / SIZING

How should it divide?

Scope estimates and recommendations for phases or child RFCs.

Provider result

The model response and its event record.

Command outcome

Response acceptance and the actual process exit.

Engineer judgment

Confirmed, refuted and unresolved findings.

The worked Validate example

Trace the held-payment checkout finding through the PRD, design and RFC. Recheck historical findings against current sources; keep both F2 scenarios in scope.

Audit recommends splits; the engineer reviews and authors them.

Verify / Check + repair

Check the meaning.

TraceabilityInspect spec-to-task mapping and any unavailable checks.
Plan VerifierCoverage, relationships and semantic drift against every planned document.
Fix AgentRepairs tasks and the contract; review before/after changes.
Human decisionsUnresolved design or policy returns to its owner and the appropriate earlier stage.

CLI path in the pinned SPEED build

speed plan --feature adaptive-auth speed verify --feature adaptive-auth

Keep the coverage claim precise

Plan reads every product, design, architecture and tech document of the feature and audits each before the Architect runs. Verify checks the tasks against every planned document. Audit on its own takes one file at a time.

Plan replaces task records in its namespace; Verify can edit them. Preserve snapshots and reports. Record gates that warned, failed or did not run. An explicit parsed pass applies to the input scope actually checked.

arula07 / Handoff and trainer pickup

A useful ending is an honest starting point

Make the next action clear.
Keep its owner visible.

Participants leave able to explain what the documents decided, what the tools checked and what remains open before commissioning implementation.

The technical packet

Sources + decisions

Both reviewed RFCs, source revisions, accepted decisions, coverage and unresolved dependencies.

The evidence packet

Plan + checked scope

Audit reports, tasks, available contract, gate results and reviewed Verify repairs.

When ready

Hand off the stated scope, acceptance methods and dependencies. Execute owns implementation and its verification.

When blocked

Name the missing decision or failed gate, its evidence, owner and next action.
